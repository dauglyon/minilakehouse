# minilakehouse — plan for the open work, 2026-10-24

Order agreed 2026-10-24. Each item points to the design that holds its changes; this file adds
what the design does not: dependencies, rules that hold until a later item lands, checks and
the docs each item updates. An item is committed and pushed when its checks pass, and the next
starts after review. One agent builds; a subagent is asked for first, with its count and task.

## 1. Switching sessions

Built 2026-10-24 (3acff31); `seed/check-sessions.py` passes.

Design: `PROVENANCE.md` 3 and 5.1, build step 3.

Builds `INSERT INTO governance.sessions (name) VALUES ('<name>')` from any engine. A name the
person has not used starts a session and points the login at it; a name they have used points
the login back at that session. Also `POST /sessions {name}`, as declarations have
`POST /declarations`.

Depends on nothing.

Changes:
- `governance/index_table.py`: `sessions` joins `WRITABLE`; a commit adding exactly one row
  with a non-empty `name` is accepted.
- `governance/app.py`: `index_commit` gives the sessions check the login's `sid`; the check
  creates or finds the session and moves the login's pointer (`logins`), and refuses any other
  commit. `POST /sessions` runs the same check.
- `gate/app.py`: the refusal for inserts into other `governance` tables names both writable
  tables.

Checks, `seed/check-sessions.py`:
- alice inserts `exp1`; `governance.sessions` shows `exp1` as current.
- Her next read credential is stamped `exp1` in `governance.lineage`.
- Inserting `default` points her back, and her next credential is stamped `default`.
- Two rows in one insert are refused.
- bob's `governance.sessions` does not show alice's sessions.

Docs: `PROVENANCE.md` build order and header; README's provenance section.

## 2. Roles and tenants

Built 2026-10-24; `seed/check-roles.py`, `seed/check-sessions.py`,
`seed/check-index-narrowing.py` and the README's in-network demos pass. The running stack's
governance database was migrated in place to the renamed seed (the data volumes were backed up
first), and `platform-admin` was added to the running realm; a fresh stack gets both from
`governance/registry.json` and `keycloak/realm-lakehouse.json`. Platform admins pass every check
an owner or a steward passes, which is how this build reads "act for a tenant that has none,
and act in emergencies".

Design: `docs/HANDOFF-2026-10-22-groups.md` section 1, its roles and tenants.

Builds:
- `groups` and `group_members` with `role` (owner, admin, member, viewer) and `steward`;
  `group_owners` goes.
- Stewards appointed and removed only by holders of the Keycloak realm role `platform-admin`.
- Viewers never hold `write`; grants may name `<group>#<role>`.
- The name rule and reserved names.
- `POST /groups`, `PATCH /groups/<name>`, the steward routes, `DELETE /groups/<name>`.
- The read routes `GET /me`, `GET /groups/<name>`, `GET /datasets/<id>`, `GET /tables/<id>`.
- The seed renamed (`jgi`, `projw`, `noaccess`, `bobonly`) and carol given `platform-admin`.

Depends on nothing; second by order.

Until item 3, registration stays:
- `POST /datasets` and `POST /tables` are allowed for a steward of a group owning a root above
  the prefix or namespace. The `stewards` group and its check go.
- `register_grants` and `namespace_owners` stay as the record of which group owns which root.
- `allow_change_root`: platform admins for a top-level root, stewards of an owning group below.
- Grants are named in the registration body as today; a space's default grants wait for item 3.
- Deleting a group is refused while it owns a root.

Checks, `seed/check-roles.py`, then `seed/check-index-narrowing.py` and the README demos:
- Any signed-in person creates a group and is its owner; a name outside the rule, or reserved,
  is refused.
- Owners change owners and admins; admins change members and viewers; a member changes nobody;
  a change leaving no owner is refused.
- Only a platform admin makes or removes a steward.
- A member's registration is refused; a steward's is accepted.
- A viewer is refused a file write credential and a table write that a grant to the group
  would otherwise give.
- `GET /groups/<name>` answers its viewers and above and refuses others.

Docs: README "Changing groups", "Assigning roots", the `stewards` mentions and the registration
demo; `DESIGN.md` 4.4; `WHITEPAPER.md` 4.2, 4.4, 7 (Operator), 8's group rows and 13.

## 3. Spaces, publishing and transfers

Design: `docs/HANDOFF-2026-10-22-groups.md` sections 2 and 3, and section 1's spaces, personal
spaces and transfers.

Depends on item 2. Built in five steps; each is committed when its checks and
`seed/check-index-narrowing.py` pass, and reviewed before the next.

**3a. Spaces and publishing.** Built 2026-10-24; `seed/check-publish.py`, `check-roles.py`,
`check-sessions.py`, `check-index-narrowing.py` and the README's in-network demos pass. Two
consequences of the design, built as follows. A folder is an entry from its first credential,
read or write: a read must bring in what the folder's files carry, which only a tracked
folder's listing keeps up to date. Folders nest until one is published, so a file's sources
come from every write credential whose folder holds it, not only those logged for its own
entry; a folder's grants reach the entries under it, not one at the folder itself. Trino has
`iceberg.rest-catalog.nested-namespace-enabled` on.
- Every group's space, `datasets/<group>/` and `lakehouse.<group>`, with section 1's default
  grants. `register_grants`, `namespace_owners`, `allow_register` and `allow_change_root` go.
- A folder becomes an entry at its first write credential; `published` replaces `pending`.
- Publishing: `PATCH /datasets/<id>` and `PATCH /namespaces/<id>`, by the space's stewards.
- Grants on folders and namespaces, inherited by everything under them.
- Tables published as namespaces: grants move off `table_entries`; the seed's `lakehouse.db`
  is split so each table that has grants of its own gets a namespace of its own.
- Checks, `seed/check-publish.py`: a member writes a folder that has no entry, and it is listed
  unpublished to the group only; a steward publishes it and a member's publish is refused; a
  namespace grant reaches a table created after it.

**3b. Credentials.** Built 2026-10-24; `seed/check-credentials.py` and the checks above pass.
The broker's field is `folder`, as the handoff names it.
- Recursive by default, `"recursive": false` for the files directly in a folder, and the
  refusal for a recursive request over a folder with a published dataset under it.
- Checks, `seed/check-credentials.py`: the cases section 3 tried on Ceph (read and write
  directly in the folder; refused inside a subfolder and when listing below it); a recursive
  request refused with the message.

**3c. Personal spaces.**
- The group `u_<user>` at a person's first appearance; the personal-space exemption and
  `revoked` (`PROVENANCE.md` 9); a declaration marking a space's folder unused shown to its
  group's admins.
- Checks, `seed/check-personal.py`: alice's own copy stays readable to her after she loses
  `read` on its source and closes once the source's stewards add her to `revoked`; no grant in
  a personal space can name anyone else.

**3d. Transfers.**
- Files by the broker's server-side copy, tables by `CREATE TABLE … AS SELECT` and
  `POST /transfers`; the `moved_to` record; the moved folder's refusal.
- Checks, `seed/check-transfers.py`: a personal folder moved to a group keeps its sources; data
  built from the old folder opens to the group; the refusal for the old path names the new one
  only to people who may describe it; a viewer's transfer is refused.

**3e. Stored sources.**
- Each entry's sources kept and updated when its folder is listed or a declaration is
  accepted, instead of worked out on every decision.
- Checks: the checks of 3a to 3d give the same decisions with stored sources.

Docs: README's registration and ingest sections and the Flow B demo; `seed/ingest-demo.py`
writes, then publishes; "publish" for "register" in README, DESIGN and the whitepaper;
`WHITEPAPER.md` 4.1, 9 and 13; `PROVENANCE.md` 6.6 and 9.

## 4. Collections

Design: `docs/HANDOFF-2026-10-22-groups.md` section 5.

Builds collections that hold datasets and other collections by reference, with `describe`,
`read`, `manage`, `write` and `delete`; `describe` and `read` reach every member; adding a
member takes its `manage` holders' consent.

Depends on item 3: collections hold published datasets.

Checks, `seed/check-collections.py`:
- `read` on a collection reads a member through it, still cut by the member's sources.
- Adding takes `write` on the collection and `manage` on the member; a cycle is refused.
- Removing a member takes away what the collection gave.
- The dataset index lists the collection to those who may describe it.

Docs: `WHITEPAPER.md` 4 and 13; README.

## 5. Drafted declarations

Design: `docs/HANDOFF-2026-10-22-groups.md` section 4.

Builds Trino's HTTP event listener reporting each completed write's inputs and output to
governance, `governance.drafts`, and confirming a draft as a declaration.

Depends on nothing built later; last by order.

First, section 4's "To verify" list: TLS client certificates on the listener; base tables
behind views and CTEs in `ioMetadata.inputs`; `ioMetadata.output` for `CREATE TABLE AS` and
`INSERT`; matching a statement to the access log when a person runs statements concurrently.

Checks, `seed/check-drafts.py`:
- A `CREATE TABLE … AS SELECT` reading `t1` produces a draft marking `t1` used and the
  session's other reads unused.
- Confirming the draft runs every check an insert into `governance.derivations` runs.
- A draft alone changes no one's access.

Docs: `PROVENANCE.md` 5.3; README.
